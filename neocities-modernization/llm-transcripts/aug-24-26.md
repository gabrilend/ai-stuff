## 2. Mobile — your instinct about the font is correct, and the source is innocent

Three things compound:


**No viewport declaration.** Not one poem-facing page has `<meta
name="viewport">` — only the source-code browser does. Without it, mobile
browsers assume a 980-pixel-wide desktop viewport, lay the page out for that,
then scale the whole thing down to the physical screen. That alone makes the
site tiny, and it also leaves the browser's automatic text-inflation feature
switched on, which the viewport tag would disable.


**No text-size-adjust.** Absent everywhere. iOS Safari and Chrome on Android
both inflate text they judge to be body copy, and they compute the inflation per
block. Blocks that inflate differently no longer share a character cell width.


**The real column-shearer: glyph fallback.** Your layout rests entirely on the
Box Drawing range — 72,157 of them on the Linux page alone, versus 12 emoji
and nothing else. The font stack names Hack, Fira Code, JetBrains Mono,
Cascadia, Consolas, Monaco, Liberation Mono, Courier New, then generic
`monospace`. **A phone has none of those installed**, so it lands on generic
monospace every time. Font substitution then happens *per character*, not per
run: the Latin letters come from the monospace face, but any box-drawing
character the face lacks is pulled from a different font — typically a
proportional symbols font with a wider advance. The rare junctions you use
(`╘` `╧` `╠` `╦` `╩` `┤`) are the ones most likely to be missing
even from fonts that cover the common `─` and `│`, which is exactly why only
*some* characters land at the wrong column.


One aggravating detail: `<b>` is applied to box-drawing characters **and nothing
else**. Every horizontal bar is bold for its filled portion and regular for its
unfilled portion. If the substituted fallback font has no real bold face, the
browser synthesizes one — and synthetic bold does not reliably preserve
advance width. So each bar is asked to keep two different faces in the same
grid.

## (5) Why they're all 83% similar — it's the model, and I can prove it isn't your poems

```
poems  vs colour anchors  cos = 0.943

words  vs colour anchors  cos = 0.995

```


They all point the same way. And the single words are **more** concentrated than

It's a known property called **anisotropy**, or representation degeneration.

that sum carries a large component that has nothing to do with the input —
plus mean-pooling over tokens means the punctuation, function words, and
start-of-sequence token that appear in *every* text contribute to *every*
vector.

is a within-population comparison. Safe.


Word-against-poem is not. Single words carry a 0.947 shared component; poems
carry 0.827. The offset differs between the two sides, so it stops being
constant and starts distorting the ordering. Measured: the word "linux" alone
