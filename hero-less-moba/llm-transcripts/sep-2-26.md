### Assistant Response 6

**There is no pathfinding in this game, in the sense of a search.** No A*, no
flow field, no per-tick route computation. Every route a body could ever take
was decided when the map was built, and a body's whole idea of "where am I
going" is *one number that goes up*. Everything below is either that number, or
a correction applied to it.

That is the design decision the entire movement system hangs off, so it's worth
stating what it buys: with a thousand bodies on the field, a per-body route
search is the whole frame budget. Here, advancing a body is an addition and a
table lookup.

---

## 1. The ground, decided once

The map is generated from shape parameters at load — there is no map file and
no editor, deliberately, because a hand-tweaked map can't be regenerated and a
map nobody dares regenerate is a map nobody dares change.

What it produces is a **path graph**: a flat array of nodes, and three lanes
that each name an ordered walk through them.

**A node** (a Lua table, all fields always present, never nil):

| Field | Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id` | integer | its own index in the array |
| `x`, `y` | double | position in paces — used for **drawing and weapon range only**, never for deciding where to walk |
| `kind` | integer | 1 plain, 2 junction, 3 tower site, 4 base spawn, 5 library site |
| `lane` | integer | 1–3, or 0 for a connector between lanes |
| `milestone` | integer | 0–8 if this node is a milestone, else 0 |
| `team` | integer | 0, 1 or 2 — which half of the field it's in |
| `neighbour` | array of integers | node ids one step away |
| `structure` | integer | id of the tower or library standing here, or 0 |

**A lane** is the precomputation that makes movement cheap. Its important
fields:

| Field | Type | What it removes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `path` | array of node ids | the route itself, ordered **team 1's library to team 2's** |
| `path_index` | node id → integer | a body joining a lane never scans for its position |
| `step_length` | array of doubles | distance from each path node to the next — so the move pass **never computes a square root** |
| `cumulative` | array of doubles | how far along the lane each path node sits |
| `length` | double | total |
| `milestone_node` / `milestone_index` | integer arrays, 0–8 | where the nine landmarks are |
| `zone` / `waypoint_zone` | doubles, 0–32 | the same lane measured four times more finely; what push depth is counted in and what a waypoint sits inside |
| `width` | double | paces across |
| `files` | integer | how many bodies walk abreast |

One geometric fact makes the builder short: **milestone 4 is exactly the bend.**
The two libraries are mirror images about the junction diagonal, so a side
lane's two legs are equal, so the bend lands at exactly half the lane's length.
Every consecutive pair of milestones is therefore joined by a *straight line*,
and placing nodes between them is linear interpolation rather than walking a
polyline by arc length.

---

## 2. Where a body is — two coordinate systems, and only one of them is the truth

This is the part most likely to surprise you, and it's the root of several bugs
I hit today.

**For a body on a lane, its position is two numbers**, and its world position is
*derived*:

| Field | Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `lane_along` | double | how far down the lane it has got, in paces |
| `lane_across` | double | how far to one side of the lane's centre line it stands |
| `facing` | integer | +1 or −1 — team 1 walks **up** the path array, team 2 walks **down** it |
| `x`, `y` | double | **derived** from the two above against the lane's curve |
| `path_index`, `node_from`, `node_to` | integer | also derived |
| `progress` | double | kept roughly current for anything still reading it; nothing on a lane depends on it any more |

Writing into `x` and `y` for a lane body therefore does nothing lasting — the
next move re-derives them and the change vanishes. That's exactly the shape of
the "nudge" primitive I had to add for the separation pass.

**Holding position this way is what makes a rank survive a corner.** Every body
in a rank shares one distance-along, so the lane's own curve carries the whole
line round the bend together, for free. Held in world coordinates instead, a
turning rank either tears apart or scythes through the inside of the turn.

**For a body with no lane** — a tower guard — the truth is different: it's
on the edge between two nodes, that far along, plus `offset_x` and `offset_y` in
world paces. That offset pair exists because a graph edge is a line with no
width: every guard on the same edge at the same progress is at the *same point*.
Two guards at one tower stood inside each other for a whole match while the
anti-overlap rule ran every tick and achieved nothing, because it was writing
into world position and the next pass re-derived it.

**One path array, read two ways.** Folding `facing` into the index arithmetic is
why there's one array rather than two — and why every "how far along am I"
comparison in the project multiplies by facing.

---

## 3. The four ways of moving

Which node comes next is a dispatch on what the body is doing, not a branch
inside the move loop. All four are "read one number out of a table"; none
searches.

| Mode | Used by | Next node is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| lane | everything with a lane | the next entry in the lane's path array |
| wander | a guard on patrol | a random neighbour still inside its leash |
| home | a guard leashing back | whichever neighbour is nearer its tower |
| toward | a guard closing on something | whichever neighbour is nearer its target |

The greedy nearest-neighbour step in the last one is correct *only* because a
guard chases only inside its own leash radius, and the graph in there is a
corridor. A guard that needed a real search would already have wandered further
from its tower than it's allowed to be.

Two details that are load-bearing: the **remainder is carried** when a fast body
crosses more than one node in a tick (truncating would make speed upgrades
quietly stop paying above a threshold nobody wrote down), and the crossing loop
is **bounded at eight steps** rather than a bare loop — a body that somehow
can't advance would otherwise freeze the frame.

Reaching the end of a lane is not an error. The next-node lookup returns 0,
meaning the body is standing at the enemy library; it stops and lets targeting
find the structure.

---

## 4. One tick, in order

The tick is an ordered array of named stages. The ones that touch position:

```
clear  think  record  commands  spawn
index  form  retarget  move  index  separate
attack  resolve  reap  measure  phase  snapshot  log
```

- **index** — drop every living body into the spatial grid, so "who is near
  me" isn't a scan over every other body. Also records the largest radius
  actually on the field.
- **form** — every wave advances its anchor and decides its gears (below).
- **retarget** — anyone without a living target looks for one.
- **move** — the brain, once per living body.
- **index again, then separate** — the field is re-indexed because everybody
  just moved, and then anyone left inside anyone else is pushed apart.

That repeated index is the only duplicated row in the table, and it's there
because a separation pass reading the pre-move grid would miss exactly the pairs
that moved into each other this tick — which is all of them.

---

## 5. The brain decides *which kind* of moving

The state field indexes an array of behaviour functions, one per state, each
returning next tick's state. There is no conditional chain deciding what a
soldier is doing — the soldier already knows.

| # | State | Movement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | walking | advance, unless the rank ahead blocks |
| 2 | closing | keep advancing until the target is in weapon range |
| 3 | fighting | stop |
| 4 | leashing | guards only: walk back to the leash node, **refusing to acquire anything on the way** |
| 5 | dying | bookkeeping |
| 6, 7 | waiting, recovering | rows exist so the table is the whole list of states, not the list of built ones |

Inside the walking state there's a priority order that's worth reading as a
list, because it is the actual routing policy:

1. **A guard** never marches — it walks the graph directly and belongs to no
   formation.
2. **A body going home** during a calm walks backwards down its lane and
   vanishes on arrival, refunding what it cost.
3. **The Eternal Golem** walks and does nothing else — no acquisition, no
   closing, no fighting.
4. **A hero mid-crossing** between lanes is committed to the connector.
5. **Anything with a target** drops to closing.
6. **A hero with a turn left** consults the sign-post at its junction.
7. **A ranged body with no shot** fans out looking for an angle.
8. **A hero with no wave** walks its lane alone — which is what makes it
   fragile in a way a wave body isn't, and part of what the purchase buys.
9. **Everything else** steps toward its place in its formation.

---

## 6. The formation layer — where a marching body actually wants to be

A wave leaves the library already in its ranks; there is no forming-up moment.

A body's place is stored as a **bearing and a distance from the formation's
centre** — 0 is dead ahead, a quarter turn is the flank, half a turn is the
rear — because every question asked about a place is angular. *Can this body
shoot past that one? Is it on the flank or in the middle where the heavy troops
belong? Which way should it face?* None of those is a row and a column.

The wave carries an **anchor**: how far down the lane its *front* has got. A
body's target position is the anchor plus its own offsets, converted through the
lane's curve. Three things add to decide how far across the road it stands: its
place in its own rank, the shift that puts its whole formation abreast of the
other two during a challenge, and the wander.

**Gears, not a dial.** A body that's ahead of its place walks at 0.70 pace; a
body at or behind its place marches at full pace. **Nothing ever exceeds
marching pace** — a formation dresses itself by the inside of a turn slowing,
not the outside sprinting, which is what a body of troops actually does. A dead
band of a couple of paces stops a body a hair out of place oscillating between
gears forever.

**And the front waits.** When the formation has fallen more than half a rank
behind its own anchor, the anchor stops advancing. That's what replaces
hurrying: a body on the outside of a bend has further to walk and *cannot* make
it up, so the front stops asking.

**The wander** is the only randomness in routing. A road divides lengthways into
three columns; a wave picks one on the way out and **keeps it the whole march**,
drawing a fresh destination inside that column each time it crosses a zone
boundary and drifting toward it at a tenth of marching pace. The commitment is
the important half — a wave drawing an independent offset every zone crosses
the road repeatedly, which isn't an army with an approach, it's an army that
can't make up its mind. Each wave has **its own random stream**, seeded from the
match seed, its team and its own number; a shared stream would make one wave's
path depend on how many others were walking, which turns the wander into an
amplifier for any difference between two machines.

---

## 7. The step itself, and the three corrections applied to it

For a marching body, one tick is:

1. Ask the formation where this body's place is, in lane coordinates.
2. Take the gap along and the gap across. Clamp the along-gap to this tick's
   pace; clamp the across-gap to **0.55 of pace** — sliding back into file
   costs forward progress, so a wave bent by a turn visibly takes a moment to
   straighten rather than snapping.
3. **The refusal.** Convert the intended landing point to world coordinates and
   ask whether anything is standing there.
4. **The speed cap.** Measure how far the body actually moved *in the world* and
   scale the step back if it exceeded its own pace.

**The refusal** is one circle test: if any body's centre is within the sum of
the two radii of the ground the next step lands on, that ground snaps to the
point on the obstacle's circle nearest us. Three properties of it matter:

- It's asked about **the step, not the destination**. A formation's slot can be
  ninety paces away and whether somebody's standing on it is a question about
  the future. Asked about the slot, bodies get pushed off their places for
  obstacles they're nowhere near, formations stop dressing, and — because the
  anchor waits for stragglers — waves stop advancing.
- It pushes **radially out of the obstacle**, not back along our own path.
  Measured from our own centre instead, the correction has no sideways component
  at all when walking dead at somebody.
- **One obstacle per call**, the deepest. Resolving all at once means iterating
  toward a fixed point that need not exist — three bodies around a gap one
  body wide have no answer.

**The speed cap** corrects the one thing lane coordinates get wrong. Holding a
formation in lane coordinates makes a turn free, but the body on the *outside*
of a bend has further to walk in the world and nothing was telling it so — it
covered that extra ground for nothing, moving faster than its own speed,
silently. It's three fixed passes rather than a solve, because displacement is
monotonic in the fraction and a fixed pass count keeps the cost the same every
tick.

---

## 8. Two rules about room, answering different questions

|  | The refusal | The queue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| asks | may I stand here | should I push past the man in front of me |
| about | everything on the field, enemies included | my own side, in my own file |
| distance | the two bodies' radii — about 7 paces | a rank's spacing — 18, or 11 for a body with a reach |
| answer | go **there** instead | **wait** |

The first is physical, the second tactical. Deleting the queue and keeping only
the refusal was built and measured: bodies press to touching, a losing wave
spreads out and is killed piecemeal instead of stiffening into a block, and
matches stopped running their phase arc.

The queue is a **melee** idea. A ranged body doesn't want the front and never
did, so it holds at its own reach behind the rank with a smaller bubble. The
consequence is that a lane's depth is informative: a wave that has lost its
melee rank but kept its archers is about to evaporate, and it looks different
from one that has lost everything.

"Ahead" is a comparison, not a distance — path index plus progress across the
current edge, multiplied by facing so both directions fold into one test. It is
never used as a distance, because the steps are only roughly even.

---

## 9. The separation pass — what I added today

The refusal is preventive, and three things get past it: a cleared step is
shortened afterwards by the speed cap and lands *short* of the circle it aimed
at; only the deepest of several obstacles is resolved; and a body that isn't
moving is never asked.

So after everybody moves, every pair still overlapping is pushed apart by
exactly the overlap, split in inverse proportion to their **area** — a soldier
walking into a Golem moves, the Golem barely does. It gathers contacts in one
walk of the spatial grid, relaxes that flat list until a round finds nothing,
then **looks again**, and only leaves after a look that finds nobody overlapping
— never straight after a push.

Together the two rules hold something neither does alone: across a whole
8000-tick match, at every tick, no two bodies overlap.

It also turned out to be the first thing in the game that can move a body
**sideways out of a file**. The refusal has no sideways in it head-on;
separation runs on the same lines but between bodies already touching in a
crowd, where those lines point every way at once.

---

## 10. The only steering a player has

Waves cannot be rerouted at all. **Waves are the map's skeleton; heroes move
across it.** If waves could be redirected, a team could feed two lanes into one
and the lane structure of the map would be decorative.

A sign-post stands at each lane's junction. A hero arriving takes the branch it
points at, and **then goes straight on at every junction for the rest of its
life**, whatever the next sign says. That single rule is what keeps this from
being a routing system — no looping a body around the anti-diagonal, no
chaining two posts to reach the far lane.

The junction window is a body's **own speed**, not a fixed distance, so a fast
hero can't step over the junction between two ticks and miss the sign — which
would be a silent failure of the only steering there is.

---

## Where it is thin

- **A guard's chase is greedy** and only correct inside a leash radius. Nothing
  checks that the leash graph is actually a corridor.
- **Bodies can leave the road.** The road's width is room, not a fence —
  deliberately — but two columns squeezing past each other put a body 67.5
  paces from the centre line of a road whose half-width is 66. That's an open
  failing on the crossing scene.
- **The hero spawner drops bodies seven paces inside other bodies.** Separation
  cleans it up now, but the rule says bodies may never be *born* in the same
  place, and that spawner breaks it at source.
- **The arena has no walls.** Bodies reaching the end of a test road clamp
  against it.
