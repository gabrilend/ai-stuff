021 — Nobody Remembers Why

The setting. First pass, and deliberately thin — it exists to give the mechanics

somewhere to stand, not to be a novel. Everything here should be checkable

against something already in the design; where it is not, it is marked.

The premise

An ancient, automated war that nobody alive started.

The bases still spawn soldiers because the machinery that spawns soldiers still

works. The towers still shoot because that is what towers do. Nothing has

required a decision to keep happening for a very long time, and it has kept

happening anyway.

This is the setting the game's own subtraction produced. Take a lane-pusher and

remove the heroes — the individuals, the named people, the ones with reasons —

and what remains is exactly this: process without anybody in it. The premise

was never invented for the game; it was excavated from what the game already is.

The libraries

Each base holds one, and each holds the records of why the war began.

Nobody has read them.

Destroying the enemy's library is how you win, and it is also how the answer is

lost for good. A team that wins has not learned anything. It has burned the last

copy of the only question worth asking, and it has done so on purpose, at speed,

with everything it had.

That is why the win condition is a library and not a throne, and it is why the

library has so little health — about one and a half guard towers. It is not

fortified because nobody thinks it is important. It is a room full of paper at

the back of a machine that has been running for centuries.

The commanders

Not bodies on the map. Not people you can point to. A commander is a **name a

resource goes by and a roster of things that can be built**, and that is all

the game ever knows about one.

Read against the setting, that is the point rather than a limitation. The

commanders are whatever is still making decisions — six of them, one per player,

distant, never seen. They do not appear on the field because there may not be

anything to appear.

What the upgrades are

A wave is wiped out and something goes into your chest. The vision calls it "a

big treasure chest."

The working reading: salvage, or what you learned from watching them die. You

took a formation apart, and now the next batch out of your base is built slightly

differently. Nobody decided this. The machinery adjusts.

It is worth noticing that upgrades cannot be bought, traded, or chosen — only

earned by destruction and then arranged. The only creative act in the entire

setting is deciding where things go.

The three monsters

Three times a match something comes out of the middle. They are always the same

three, in the same order: the Pillar Orc, the Field Dragon, and **the

Eternal Golem**.

They are not summoned and nobody controls them. They are older than the war and

they are not part of it. The war is a thing happening on top of them.

The Eternal Golem

The third one, the last one, and the one that ends every match. Quoted as it was

first written, because it is the most important sentence anybody has written

about this game:

It doesn't die! It's eternal! It's deathless! It's the same golem in every

game, watching you play and learning how you fight! Too bad it can't do much

about it because it's made out of [redacted].

Take that literally and it settles a great deal.

It is the same Golem in every match. Not a new instance of a monster type —

the Golem, the one from last time, and the time before. It is not re-created at

the third surge; it arrives.

It watches, and it learns. It has been present for every match ever played.

It knows how you fight.

And it cannot do anything with what it knows, because of what it is made of.

That last line is the setting's whole thesis compressed into a joke. In a world

where nobody remembers why, **the Golem is the only thing that remembers

everything** — and it is built out of something that prevents it from acting on

the memory. It is the perfect archive and the perfectly useless one, and it is

walking toward the imperfect archive that everybody is dying over.

What it is made of is not written down. It should stay not written down.

Mechanically the Golem does not learn anything, and no part of the simulation

consults a previous match. The fiction is that it does. Those two facts should

be allowed to sit next to each other; making the Golem actually adapt would

require the game to remember matches, which is a large feature in service of a

line that works better unimplemented.

What this settles, and what it does not

Settled enough to build against:

  • The libraries are archives nobody has read, and winning destroys one.
  • The commanders are absent, and that is deliberate.
  • The monsters predate the war and are indifferent to it.
  • The Golem is singular, persistent, observant, and inert.

Not settled, and on the open questions page:

  • What the soldiers are. Conscripts, constructs, something the bases make?
  • What a boon is, given that one arrives after every surge from nowhere in

particular.

  • Why a siege-surge happens at all, given that it happens on a schedule. Something

scheduled it. Nobody remembers that either, presumably.

  • What the Golem is made of. See above; the answer may be that there is no answer.
