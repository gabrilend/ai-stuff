# spoken while building

Kept verbatim, in the order said, unedited. These are the person's words
during the making of this program. Several of them are load-bearing — the
price mechanism and the crossover measurement both come straight out of
what is written here — and the rest is kept because it was said.

Append only.

## at the start, handing over the vision

> hi can you build this project for me? feel free to use llama.cpp. we have
> a cluster of 3 that we can run it on, a local GPU, and a remote
> workstation.

> internet idea: pass it along, be paid. process it, give away. build many
> ways to process, make them open when paid, and tune the pricess
> algorithmically to equalize pressure. suddenly, everyone can use the mega
> cluster, because everyone gets out of it what they use. "Hi Amazon, I'd
> like to use this raspberry pi for the mega cluster. I can install it like
> this." great thanks for improving the megacluster, here's dollars for your
> service. "hey government can I get some dollars so that I can encourage
> people to increase innovation and [thriving rates, but pronounced
> thrive]?"

> every problem has a curve point that points to when it is more efficient
> to use fewer threads, than if you were to use more. I try and design
> software that pushes the "use more threads" benefit curve as close to "use
> more threads" as I can. Infinite scalability is a myth, but the ceiling is
> quite far away. and along the way, there are much more valuable insights
> to see. at a certain point it's faster to run the program on your computer
> than on a different thread - when that point is reached, or when we can
> achieve it, then infinite possibility space because plausible, as people
> shift into the world of ideas, where all their thoughts come to life. the
> last human step toward this, before achieving it and possibly growing
> beyond if they please at what time and what order they please, is
> programmable matter. the most crucial major innovation, which blends the
> worlds of [idea, but pronounced myth]s and matter. thus, magic forevermore.

> thank you, dinosaurs, for free will. it would be impossible without you.

**Where these went.** The price paragraph became the routing rule —
`strategems/price-as-a-load-balancer.md` and issue `402`. The curve-point
paragraph became `405`, the crossover measurement, and it is the reason
this machine is listed in the roster as a door like any other: the
crossover stops being a constant somebody tuned and becomes a comparison
made per unit, live. The last two lines are kept as said.

## partway through the survey

> bullshit machine, reverse that for me if you please

Run down the ladder, it comes back as roughly *"honest growth"* — but the
better answer is the one the ladder gives at rung 4, where clause order
swaps and each half inverts:

    bullshit  machine
    machine   bullshit
    grown     true

A machine that makes bullshit, turned around, is a growth that makes truth.
Which is a fair description of what is being built here — the mirror exists
so a person can hold a sentence next to its opposite and *tell how it makes
them feel*, and that is a bullshit detector with the sign flipped.

The line is kept because it is also the first real test case: two words,
one clause, and the ladder handles it without a special case.
