# Roadmap — six clusters of functionality

Phases group related machinery; they are not a schedule. Lower-numbered
issues within a phase are the foundations; the highest is the capstone
that proves the phase by producing something you can watch. Every phase
ends with a demo gif (or gallery) generated by a script in
`issues/completed/demos/`, and the project root gains a picker script
that runs any completed phase's demo.

## Phase 1 — The canvas and the file

Pixels exist, and they can leave the building. A floating-point light
canvas, tone-mapping to bytes, the purpose-built glow palette, and a
handwritten GIF89a encoder with LZW. Proven by the first light: a
glowing dot orbiting on black, looping in a browser.

Issues: 101 project skeleton and run harness · 102 light canvas and
tone-mapping · 103 glow palette and indexing · 104 gif encoder ·
105 first-light demo (capstone).

## Phase 2 — Particle life

The pool, the emitters, the physics, the glow. Preallocated parallel-
array particle pool with swap-with-last reaping; emitter recipes with
fractional spawn accumulation; drag, jitter, and fade; the additive
radial splatter. Proven by a burst: a firework bloom and a fountain,
rendered to gif.

Issues: 201 particle pool · 202 emitters · 203 particle physics ·
204 glow rendering · 205 burst demo (capstone).

## Phase 3 — Choreography

Time learns to steer. Parametric paths with the clock-face vocabulary
(arcs, lines, points), the easing library ("stroke": slow then fast),
timeline tracks with activation windows, and fill-regions whose
coverage grows. Proven by the vision itself, choreographed directly in
code: two clocks sweeping inward, a line fading in between their
resting points, the triangle filling slowly.

Issues: 301 parametric paths · 302 easing library · 303 timeline
tracks · 304 fill regions · 305 two-clocks demo (capstone).

## Phase 4 — The scene language

The choreography gets a front door. The score — a flat todo list of
stroke calls, each with its time in tenths, its color, its shape, its
fade envelope, sorted by a canonical writer — plus the compiler that
turns a score into a timeline (with its wall of named validation
errors), the command-line runner that reads `input/` and writes
`output/`, and the HTML gallery that views what was made — generation
and viewing kept strictly apart. Proven end-to-end: the vision prose
translated into a score shipped in `input/`, rendered by one command.

Issues: 401 scene script format · 402 scene compiler · 403 command-line
runner · 404 html gallery viewer · 405 vision render (capstone).

## Phase 5 — Many hands

The pipeline learns to use every core. Frame snapshots fan out to
worker threads for rasterizing and LZW compression while the simulator
keeps ticking (effil Lua threads, or the house C threadpool over FFI —
decided by measurement, not taste); a statistics utility reports
frames-per-second, particles-per-frame, and bytes-per-frame so the
documentation can point at a tool instead of at stale numbers. Proven
at scale: a large-canvas render with single-thread and many-thread
timings side by side.

Issues: 501 parallel render pipeline · 502 render statistics utility ·
503 big-canvas demo (capstone).

## Phase 6 — The listening porch

Prose learns to knock. A plain-English `.prose` file in `input/` is
read to small local models — llama.cpp behind an orchestrator — which
paint in tool-calls, one per stroke, constrained by a grammar
*generated from the score vocabulary itself*, so a misspelled or
misremembered word can describe a mechanism and still land on the
legal name for it; the collector orders the strokes by their declared
times. Three independent readings; each faces the ordinary validation
wall (its errors quoted back for bounded retries), renders as a fast
low-resolution thumbnail, and stands beside the original prose on a
pick page. The person chooses; the chosen scene is promoted into
`input/` as a first-class citizen. Proven by speaking: a prose
paraphrase of the vision — with a deliberate misspelling in it — goes
in, and the gesture comes out.

Issues: 601 prose-to-scene translator · 602 three readings ·
603 spoken-vision demo (capstone).

## Phase 7 — One canvas, two spaces

The house's art tools had grown apart: this studio painted 2D light, a
second studio in delta-version filmed 3D shapes using this one's encoder
and palette by a borrowed path, a viewer beside it held the only complete
GIF decoder, and a skill described studios in general. She asked for one:
*"The gif generator should be able to generate 2d images using it's limited
vocabulary, and the 3d shape generation should be describable in an
expanded 3d vocabulary. We should be able to paint both onto the same
canvas, with 2d using screen-space coordinates and 3d using world-space
coordinates."* The shape films and the viewer move here with their history;
the 3D words become a closed language with the same wall; a score holds
both, the 3D layer drawn through its camera, the 2D layer as light behind
or over it, composed into one frame and one palette; one encoder, one
decoder; every film carries a rated card; one skill says how to use it.
Proven by the six approved films re-filming byte-identical at every step,
and by a mixed scene where a stroke of light follows a shape through the
world.

Issues: 701 one studio, one canvas, two spaces, in parts: 701a bring the
shape films home · 701b one reader, one writer · 701c the 3D vocabulary ·
701d one canvas, two spaces · 701e every film kept and rated · 701f the
studio skill · 701g two spaces demo (capstone).
