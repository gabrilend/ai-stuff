# spoken while building

The person's own words, kept verbatim, in the order they were said. Nothing
here is paraphrased and nothing here is tidied. When a phrase turns out to be
load-bearing, the document that leans on it points back to this file.

---

> hiiiiiii can you create this project with me? pink star squiggles!

*(the greeting that opened the project, 31 July 2026)*

**pink star squiggles** arrived before any of the four colour schemes in the
vision had been chosen, and before a single line of the client existed. It is
the first thing said about how the world should *look*, and it is three words
that each name a different layer of the same picture:

- **pink** — a hue, and one that appears in none of the four schemes written
  down in `notes/vision`. White-bright, black-bright, blue-muted, green-muted.
  Pink is the fifth thing, or it is the thing that crosses all four.
- **star** — a shape with points, radial, made of straight lines meeting at a
  centre. The vision asks for "lots of geometry and shapes"; a star is the
  geometry that reads as *light* rather than as *architecture*.
- **squiggles** — the opposite of geometry. A hand-drawn wandering line. The
  only word in the vision's neighbourhood that is not made of straight edges.

A world of squares and triangles, and the things alive in it are pink, are
stars, and squiggle. The terrain is rigid and the inhabitants are not. That
contrast is being treated as the visual thesis of the project until the person
says otherwise.

---

> **Pink versus multicolour** — *"boy vs girl?"*

Asked as a question, answered as one. The reply does not pick a side; it names
the *shape* of what was asked and declines it. Pink-or-multicolour was posed as
a fork, and the answer points out that it is the same kind of fork as boy-or-
girl: a binary laid over something that was never two things.

So the design stops choosing. **The whisp is pink and it is multicolour**, and
those are not competing claims about the same property. Pink is the form — the
shared, immediately recognisable fact of being a living thing here. The colour
lives *inside* that form rather than beside it: the hue drifts through a range
with pink at its centre, different per creature, never leaving.

Which means there is no palette entry called "the whisp colour" and no second
entry called "the other whisp colour." There is a centre and a wander, exactly
like the geometry has a root and a wander. The same shape twice, which is
usually the sign that a design has found its own grain.

---

> **Which camera** — *"if it's my camera, it's a witch camera, thank you very much."*

Which → witch, and the question falls over. It had offered three rigs to choose
between: overhead, isometric, over-the-shoulder. All three are *mounts* — a
camera bolted to a position and dragged along behind a player.

A witch camera is not mounted. It flies.

So the camera stops being a choice from a list and becomes **another floating
thing in a world of floating things** — it drifts, it lags, it leans into turns,
it holds still when you hold still and gets curious when you do not. It is the
one object in the design that watches rather than being watched, and it has as
much right to wander as the arms of a star do.

The three rigs remain useful as the extremes it can be pushed toward, which is a
better use for them than being voted on.

---

> **On the big numbers** — *"oh yes number waves, right? it's here:*
> `https://www.desmos.com/calculator/cxayticajg` *basically it's this:*
>
>     y = {x > 2: -sin(pi*x/2) * 2
>
> *I think part of the multiplication is just for a visual effect, but the
> visual effect is striking."*

The question asked about was a different kind of number — the modular
arithmetic that proves a password without sending one. This answered a question
nobody had asked yet, which is the more useful of the two things that can happen
when a thread is picked up sideways.

Read it as written: a wave that **does not exist below a threshold**, and which
crosses zero at exactly the threshold where it begins.

```
        y
        2 ┤        ╭─╮                 ╭─╮
          │       ╱   ╲               ╱   ╲
        0 ┼──────●─────╲─────────────●─────╲──────  x
          │      2      ╲    ╱        6     ╲   ╱
       -2 ┤              ╰──╯                ╰─╯
                 ↑
                 nothing at all, to the left of here
```

The period is four and the gate is at two, so at the moment the wave is allowed
to exist it is worth exactly zero. Move the gate to one and it would begin at
full amplitude — a cliff. **The gate is placed where the wave crosses zero**,
and that is why it emerges instead of appearing.

That is the whisp's arm, described before the whisp's arm was written down. An
arm is still at its root and free at its tip; a wander that started at full
strength at the root would make the star look broken rather than soft. The
striking thing and the correct thing turn out to be the same thing, which is the
usual reason to trust a shape somebody drew before they had a use for it.

The doubling is indeed for effect. It is an amplitude, and amplitudes belong in
`docs/balance-updates.md`, where they can be turned without touching a document.
