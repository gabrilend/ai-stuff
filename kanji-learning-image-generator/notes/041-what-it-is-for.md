# 041 — What it is for

The founding note in `notes/vision` says what to build. This says what it is
*for*, and it arrived after the first three phases were finished — in answer to
a question about whether characters that match no world should be refused or
given a default. The answer reframed the project, and it is kept here in the
words it was given in.

> um... what? we should have the scenery reflect the meaning of the kanji, and
> the radicals composing it be represented by the names they bear. I'm working
> through wanikani and I'm on chapter 2. I wanted to make a study tool.
> Something that created images for kanji or sets of characters that comprise a
> phrase or word. Then the image would illustrate what it means. so... there's a
> radical that's a sun, so the character is displayed as a sun in the context of
> the scenery. Next to it is a radical for clock, so it represents time spent
> outside - I dunno if that's a real character or not but you get the idea. And
> just... we gotta try as hard as we can, and some of them will suck but use the
> skill I just made for painting and we should be able to continually sort the
> ones that are terrible and supply them with better arguments as we please to
> try and create a new kind of dictionary

## What this changes

**Four things, and the first one reverses a decision.**

**A radical is a named thing, and it appears in the picture under its name.**
That is how the mnemonic tradition this is being learned through works: 時 is a
*sun* over a *temple*, and it is memorable precisely because a temple has
nothing to do with time.

`docs/004` demoted exactly those pieces to background scenery, on the argument
that painting the half of a character chosen for its sound puts an object in the
picture unrelated to the word. That argument is correct and it is answering a
different question. It is right for *the picture should be about the meaning*
and wrong for *the picture should be a hook you can hang the meaning on*. Phase
four is the second thing.

**A phrase is a picture too.** Characters do not only appear alone; they
combine into words, and the word is what a learner is actually trying to hold.
The machinery is built entirely around single characters.

**Some of them will be bad, and that is expected rather than a failure.** The
plan is not to get every one right. It is to make many, sort out the ones that
miss, and re-argue those with better descriptions — so the set improves by being
used.

**The goal is a dictionary.** Not a gallery of clever pictures: a reference
somebody looks a character up in.

## What it does not change

The illusion. The strokes still carry the composition, the writing order is
still the viewing order, and the arrows still say so. All of that is what makes
the picture the character rather than a picture *about* the character.
