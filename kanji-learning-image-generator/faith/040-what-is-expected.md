# 040 — What is expected

## That the etymology is the thing

The bet this project makes is that a character built out of a person and a tree
is best learned as a picture of a person and a tree — that the association a
flashcard asks you to manufacture is one the character has been carrying all
along, and that showing it is better than drilling it.

Nothing here proves that. It is the reason the archive's group tree was worth
reading properly rather than flattening, and the reason the half of a character
chosen for its sound is kept out of the picture. If the bet is wrong, the
machinery still works and the images are merely pretty.

## That the refusals hold

Every prompt refuses text, lettering, calligraphy, brush strokes and the names
of the writing systems, permanently and not as a setting. The expectation is
that this is enough to stop a model from satisfying "kanji" the lazy way, by
painting one on a wall in the scene.

If it is not enough, the failure will be quiet and it will look good, which is
why the refusal list is a constant with a comment on it rather than a knob.

## That somebody will run it on a machine with a graphics card

Six thousand recipes and no kitchen. The whole project is written on the
expectation that the far end exists, behaves the way the format says it does,
and belongs to somebody who will tell us when it does not.

## That the numbers will be turned

Every dial in `input/settings.lua` was set once, against one processor, on one
afternoon, mostly by looking at six characters. They are starting positions, and
`docs/balance-updates.md` exists so the second person to touch them can see what
the first was chasing.
