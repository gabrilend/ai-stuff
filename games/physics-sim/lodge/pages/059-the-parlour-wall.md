# The Parlour Wall

The wall by the door is covered in things about the real game, the one this
machine dreams about.

## Where it came from

Pachinko began as a children's toy. In the 1920s a vertical version of the
**Corinthian bagatelle**, an American tabletop game of pins and holes, was
sold in Japan as the "Corinth game". By about 1930 it had become an adult
pastime in Nagoya, named for the sound the balls make, and after the war the
first commercial parlour opened there in 1948. Machines of that time were
built from old tea chests and greenhouse glass.

- [Pachinko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachinko), the long story.
- [Bagatelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagatelle), the grandparent: a
  billiard table narrowed, its pins turned to pegs.
- [Pachislot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachislot), the cousin with
  reels.
- [The rolling-ball games that shaped arcades](https://nikkeivoice.ca/arcade-origins-in-the-japanese-rolling-ball-how-tamakorogashi-shaped-the-world-of-modern-gaming/),
  from tamakorogashi onward.
- [Pinballs and shopping malls](https://www.sealionpress.co.uk/post/chains-of-consequences-pinballs-and-shopping-malls-the-strangely-prophetic-history-of-pachinko),
  the strangely prophetic history of pachinko.
