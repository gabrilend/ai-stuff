# 009 — Events, and What People Know

What is hidden in the city, who holds it, and the rule that stops it becoming a
story.

## An event is a hook

One concrete, hidden, local fact. The shape of it, in the author's own words:

> there's a secret key in a box in the living room on one of the endtables that
> opens a chest in the block on the other side of the yard

Three things are doing work in that sentence.

**It is small and ordinary.** A key, a box, a chest. Not a prophecy, not a
bloodline, not a door that has waited a thousand years. Somebody put a key
somewhere sensible and somebody else does not know about it.

**It reaches across an address.** The key is in one block; the chest is in
another. An event is an **edge**, not a leaf — it joins two places that have no
reason to be joined, and the joining is the whole of its interest.

**It belongs to people, not to places.** The key does not know it is a secret.
Whoever knows about it, knows, and can act on it, and can tell somebody else.

## Knowledge is a set of held events

A person's knowledge **is** the events they hold. There is no separate knowledge
system, no discovery flags, no fog to lift.

Which means this and the per-person filters described in
[filters and the weave](006-filters-and-the-weave.md) are **one system, not two**.
A filter reading "what I know of hidden things here" is a count of the events
this person holds at this address. Nothing bridges them because there is nothing
to bridge.

It also means the thing that makes ignorance visible on the map — a filter
answering *nothing* for a block, which draws as bare painting — is answering
nothing because this person holds no events there. Blindness is not modelled. It
is just what having no events looks like.

## The rule that keeps it from becoming a story

> Just keep it from becoming a massive story. People live in cities, so what?

Written down because it is exactly the kind of constraint that erodes one
well-meaning quest chain at a time.

**Every event is local, ordinary, and consequential only nearby.** No arcs, no
chosen ones, no chains where the key opens the chest that holds the map that
names the heir. A key opens a chest. In the chest is something a person in this
block would plausibly have put there. That is the end of it.

The variety comes from there being tens of thousands of them, each written by
looking at one real place on the map and asking what would be true of it — not
from any one of them being remarkable.

## Where an event lives

Addressed by **block, building, house** — the three levels from
[the places of the city](003-the-places-of-the-city.md). Finer detail than that
lives in the event's own words: the living room, the endtable, the third shelf.
Those are prose, not structure. Nothing can query what is in a cellar, because
cellars are not a thing the program knows about, and that is a deliberate saving
rather than an oversight.

## How many, and when they get written

Two tiers, on wildly different timescales:

| | How many | At thirty a day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **one per block** | ~2,000 | **one to two months** |
| **at least one per house** | ~20,000–40,000 | **two to four years** |

So the split, which is the only version that ever ships:

**Every block gets its event before the game is played.** These are the ones the
city cannot do without — they are what makes any block worth entering.

**Houses fill in forever.** A house with no event yet simply has nothing hidden
in it. The game is playable in months and keeps deepening for a decade, and
nothing is ever blocked on the writing.

The cost is honest and worth stating: early on, most houses are empty of secrets,
and that emptiness is visible. It looks exactly like a city you have not finished
learning, which is close enough to the intended feeling that it may not read as a
gap at all.

## What is still unknown here

How an event is *acted upon* — what holding one lets you do, how it passes from
one person to another, whether telling someone costs anything. That is mechanics,
and mechanics are deliberately not decided. See
[open questions](012-open-questions.md).
