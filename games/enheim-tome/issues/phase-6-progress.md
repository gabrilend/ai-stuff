## The most blocked phase, and why that is fine

Four of ten wait on open questions, and every one of those is **a mechanics
question wearing an interface question's clothes**: what the text colours signify
is a claim about what kinds of thing exist; whether queue order shows depends on
whether a queue is a program or a set of intentions.

So they are built as containers that take their content from tables rather than
from source, and deciding them later is data rather than code.

## What the tome pays for

The map carries no text of any kind. That refusal keeps the painting undamaged and
means label collision — a genuinely hard problem producing flickering mediocre
results — never has to be solved.

**Search is what repays it.** Without a way to reach a place by name, a wordless
map is a map you can only wander.

## The two things the layout is fighting

**Any number of filters, in a welded region.** Six filters with a colour, an angle,
a mode and their own parameters is twenty rows of controls, and a fixed region
holding twenty rows is not a region, it is the whole tome. Chips bound it: twelve
filters is still one or two rows, and only the focused one spends vertical space.

**A queue is a sequence; buttons in fixed positions are not.** The pane can show
that one button has two pending and another has one, but not which happens first.
Working ruling is a count badge, pending an answer from the mechanics side.

## The idea worth keeping

**Dimming is information.** A button the place does not afford is dimmed rather
than removed, so the pattern of what is lit describes the place before a word is
read. Removing them would destroy that and break the fixed positions too.
