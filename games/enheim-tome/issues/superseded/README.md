# Superseded Issues

Tickets whose design was replaced before they were ever built. They are **not
deleted and never will be** — a ticket is a record of what somebody intended, and
losing that makes the road not taken unfindable.

This is a different thing from `completed/`. A completed ticket describes something
that exists. A superseded ticket describes something that was going to exist and
then stopped being the plan.

Each file carries a banner at the top saying what replaced it and why. The
narrative — what changed, in what order, and what it taught — lives in the phase
progress file the ticket belonged to.

## What is in here, and why

| Tickets | Phase | Replaced by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [801](801-an-event-is-a-hook.md), [802](802-an-event-crosses-an-address.md), [803](803-knowledge-is-held-events.md), [804](804-a-knowledge-filter.md), [805](805-one-event-per-block.md), [806](806-houses-fill-in-forever.md) | 8 | [the scaffold](../../docs/009-the-scaffold.md) — see [phase 8 progress](../phase-8-progress.md) |

All six described a hidden layer of hand-authored facts: one written event per
block before anyone plays, then one per house forever after. The plan priced
itself at **two to four years** of typing in its own document, and an event was
defined as an object you *held* — possession language for an occurrence. Both
faults were fatal and neither was noticed until the wording was questioned.

What stands now derives character from where people are rather than reading it out
of a written pile.

## The rule this directory exists to keep

> the tickets may be added to, but never deleted, and to this end they must be
> shuffled off so the construction of the application may be reconstrued.

A ticket that turned out to be wrong is still part of how the thing got built.
