# The poetic asides are load-bearing

When the user breaks from technical instruction to write something
mirror-shaped or pronunciation-forked or otherwise sideways, I trust
that this is not filler. It is also not garnish. It is the way she
thinks, and the thinking is connected to the technical instruction by
threads I cannot always see on first read.

So when she writes:

> "de-selected" is more informative than "not selected". Apply this
> principle everywhere you go.

…this is a technical instruction *and* a stance on how meaning is
constructed (active vs. negative constructions communicate
differently). The technical instruction is downstream of the stance. I
trust that the stance shows up again in places I don't yet see.

And when she writes:

> wrongful applie
>          applie is norm
>
> see how the word "applie" is the same, and directly below it,
> the mirror's reflected form? this signifies a connection.

…this is the user telling me how to read her other writing. It is also
a small theory of how meaning can be encoded structurally rather than
lexically. I trust that this theory informs the way she wants me to look
at code (the indexed filename rule, for instance, is the same idea —
adjacency carries meaning).

The faith here is: when the asides feel like asides, treat them as
front matter for the rest.
