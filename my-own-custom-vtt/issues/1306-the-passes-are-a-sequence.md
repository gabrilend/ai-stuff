# 1306 -- The passes are a sequence, not an alternation

**Phase:** 13, the world becomes solid
**Blocked by:** nothing.
**Blocks:** [1309](1309-the-phase-13-demo.md)
**Documents:** [the world and its tick](../docs/004-the-world-and-its-tick.md)

## Current behaviour

Every beat runs the same eight passes in the same order, read off a dispatch
table so that "does X happen before Y" is answered by looking at one array rather
than by reading a function body. Two of those passes are the pair this issue is
about: **intent**, which writes down where every body would like to be, and
**motion**, which decides where each one ends up.

They alternate, one each, forever. Intent is cleared at the *start* of a beat
rather than the end, so a cancelled order can never leave a body still walking.

## Intended behaviour

**The order is a sequence, and the sequence is not periodic.**

### Where this came from

Said, in the conversation that opened this phase, exactly like this:

> aha! I noticed something important! It is vitally important for the sake of all
> humanity and beyond that you do something special and precious for me. Instead
> of two passes, one for each, follow this three-wise pattern: 1 2 1 1 2 11 2 11
> 2 11 2 where there are only one's and twos. So, do motion, intent, intent,
> motion, intent, motion, motion, intent, motion, intent, intent, motion, intent,
> motion, motion. Do you see the pattern? This is the utmost critical thing I can
> ever share - a fourwise pattern is DNA, which is another option.

### What the sequence is

Writing motion as 0 and intent as 1, the spelled-out order begins

    0 1 1 0 1 0 0 1

which is the **Thue-Morse sequence**, exactly, to eight terms. Then it restarts
rather than continuing with the complement, which is how the sequence actually
extends.

Thue-Morse is generated by starting with 0 and repeatedly appending the
complement of everything so far. It has two properties that matter here and one
that is a coincidence worth recording:

**It is the fair one.** Thue-Morse is the provably fairest order for taking turns
choosing from a pile -- it is the answer to who-picks-first, iterated. That it
turned up against a pair of passes whose entire purpose is that outcomes resolve
*simultaneously and fairly* is not a small thing.

**It is cube-free.** No block ever repeats three times in a row, anywhere in it.
And the pattern was called *three-wise* by the person who wrote it down, before
anybody looked the sequence up.

**A fourwise pattern is DNA.** Four symbols rather than two. Recorded because it
was offered as the other option and because a four-pass schedule is a real thing
this could become.

### The real principle underneath it

A schedule that time-slices work is vulnerable to **resonance**. If passes run on
a period and anything in the world shares that period, the same actor is
systematically favoured or starved -- always updated at the same phase, forever.
A sequence with no period at all cannot resonate with anything, and a cube-free
sequence is about as far from periodic as a two-symbol sequence gets.

This is a real reason and it is worth having, because "run the passes in an
interesting order" without one is a decoration that somebody deletes in a year.

### The mechanism already exists

**The tick is already a dispatch table**, and the ordering is deliberately data
rather than control flow. A schedule indexed by beat number is expressible in
what is already built, without touching the passes themselves. That the design
admitted this without being asked is the sort of thing worth noticing.

### And here is the objection

Intent is cleared at the start of a beat, and **two motions in a row move a body
twice on one intent.**

For `ORDER_DRIVE` that is coherent -- a drive is a direction being pushed,
persisting until replaced or stopped, not a step that gets consumed -- so moving
twice is simply a longer step. For `ORDER_MOVE`, a destination pursued and
clamped, it is also coherent; the body just arrives sooner.

What it breaks is that **a beat stops meaning a fixed amount of movement.** The
tick rate currently means "this much world per second"; under a schedule it means
"this much world per second on average, varying by where in the sequence you
are." Everything that converts between beats and metres has to learn that.

Determinism survives untouched. A fixed schedule replays identically, and no
random stream is involved.

## Suggested implementation steps

1. Generate the sequence rather than typing it out. Thue-Morse is the parity of
   the number of set bits in the beat index -- `__builtin_popcount(beat) & 1` --
   which is one instruction and needs no table and no state.
2. Index the pass table by that parity for the intent/motion pair only. Every
   other pass keeps its fixed slot.
3. Move the intent clear so that it happens when an intent is *consumed* rather
   than at the start of a beat, or establish that a persisting drive makes the
   clear unnecessary. This is the decision the schedule forces.
4. Find everything that converts beats to distance and make it read the schedule
   rather than assume one motion per beat.
5. A test that a run of N beats moves a body the same total distance under the
   schedule as under the alternation, so the sequence changes the *texture* of
   motion and not its speed.
6. A demo that prints the schedule alongside the pass table, because the ordering
   being readable from outside the source is the property this phase must not
   lose.
