Frame as a Graph

The question

The first report measured uniform work: every unit's skeleton, the same job thousands of times. That is exactly what a plain parallel loop is best at, and the ceramic engine only matched it. But the engine's promise was never uniform work.

“My understanding was always that the benefit of the ceramic core engine was in its flexibility — the ability to run arbitrary tasks, not just the same type … Enabling parallelism when it is not structurally suited for it, that's the goal.”

So this test builds a frame with the shape real frames have: stages of different sizes that depend on each other, work whose length isn't known until it runs, and work in the background that doesn't belong to any frame. Every task does real work with a checkable answer, and every way of running it must agree on every frame's checksum.

Hand-written, by systemthe usual engine shape

One parallel loop per system, in order: simulation, then fog, then poses, then pathfinding, then culling, with every thread meeting at a barrier after each. Within a loop threads take the next task from a shared counter, so each loop balances itself. Background decoding gets its own dedicated thread.

Hand-written, by levelthe best a barrier design can do

Everything that could run together shares one loop: simulation; then fog, poses and pathfinding together; then culling. Three barriers instead of five. Same dedicated background thread.

Hand-written job systemthe strongest opponent

The design shipping engines use, written for this one frame. Every task is a job with a count of unfinished inputs; finishing a job releases its dependents onto the finishing thread's own queue, so a lane's culling starts the moment its poses finish, just as in the graph. An idle thread steals from the others' queues, spins a while, then sleeps. Same dedicated background thread.

Ceramic graphthe frame as a map

The renderer's thread hands the whole frame in to the task queue as one batch (tick, lane requests, pathfinding requests, and the background decodes), on the kept engine copy with the lock-free queue. Nothing says “wait for the previous stage”: fog for one player starts the moment the simulation lands, a lane's culling the moment its poses land, and a free worker takes whatever is ready.

Sleeping or spinning. The barrier ways run three ways: threads that sleep while they wait (solid bars), threads that spin — keep checking without sleeping (dense hatch), and threads that spin a while and then sleep (light hatch). It matters here more than it looks; see below. The job system and the spin-then-sleep barriers were each given their best spin length, chosen by a timed trial (listed under the method).
