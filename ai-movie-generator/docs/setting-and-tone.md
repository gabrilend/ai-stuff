# Setting and tone — the first film

The machine is general (any sound, any theme), but the vision names the film
it was built to make first, and the writers' room's standing instructions
start from it.

## The station

Modern life as a posting on a station the size of a star: going from one
room to another, corridors without windows, strip lights, badge doors,
quiet departments whose purpose is not explained. Severance's flat office
calm, laid over the Death Star's scale. (The vision's aside — that Dyson
spheres get a bad rap just for being a station the size of a star — is the
station's sympathetic side: it is enormous, and it is also home.)

## The register

Drama, not action. The daily report log: a staff member gives their report
— "today I had the strangest sith vision" — and the therapist answers,
"it's okay." Ordinary voices about extraordinary things, said plainly.
The visions are where the gif generator's particle light belongs: the one
thing on the station that is not grey.

## The people

The station's crew are a public — the vision's "common humanity is who we
are in public", the farmers market rather than the corridor. Crowds should be
varied, mixed and ordinary. The ghostsong-off-brand embeddings installed in
ComfyUI (bull-folk, dwarf, gnome, night elf, orc, undead, demon, death knight)
can staff the station too, which suits the game-soundtrack road: render the
game's people working their shift.

## Rendering a game from its soundtrack

Answered by the owner on 2026-10-28: the film shows a world **imagined from
the music's mood**, not a particular existing game. The writers' room fills
in details at will — what kind of game it feels like (a fantasy overworld, a
space-station sim, a city builder, a dungeon at night), its places, its
people, what they are doing on their shift. Every design is original: no
existing game's or film's characters, logos or named places are drawn. The
station itself is an original station that carries the *feeling* the vision
describes — vast, corridored, quietly strange — rather than any film's.
The ghostsong-off-brand embeddings fit this rule; they are the house's own
fantasy peoples.
