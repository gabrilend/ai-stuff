## What it shows

The map: a round grid of six tier rings around the hub, drawn flat then
folded so outer rings bunch toward the edge, seen through a camera ten
million units away (nearly flat, evenly scaled). Four quadrants tinted
amber, cyan, magenta and lime for seats 1 to 4. 192 units as oblong
spheroids — grey metal with a band in their seat's colour, a specular
glint — scattered and wandering; foes in dark rust, further out.

In the middle, a round brushed-metal dashboard hung on a sheet in front of
the world: arc windows showing OCTOBER and SATURDAY on paper dials, a small
hole onto the day number (10) one disc lower, three readouts (144 frames a
second, 6.9 ms a frame, 192 units), eight painted order buttons around the
rim, and a slot reading SEAT 4 - LEVEL 7 — the unit just named by a
scripted map click (it glows).


## The story so far

The vision asked for a strategy game whose controls sit in the middle of
the map, on your own base, with orders told to someone and carried out by
courier. A first design pass turned that into documents and 52 issues; the
owner then sharpened it: map click then dashboard click, dials only show
information, soramech as the engine's shape, a world that is really a
sphere, no AI-generated art. This is the first picture: the far camera,
the sphere fold, spheroid units whose positions live in shared memory and
are moved by worker threads first come first served, a framerate cap from
0 to 240, and a glass-and-metal dashboard laid on a plane sized exactly to
the view. Next, the owner asked for bodies with bending limbs and a console
shaped like one arch of an M.
