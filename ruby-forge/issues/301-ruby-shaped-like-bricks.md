## Intended Behavior

A blueprint for making ruby in the shape of bricks, in quantity. There are
two very different kinds of ruby brick, and the blueprint says which it is
making.

- **Single-crystal bricks.** Each brick is one crystal, clear and deep red,
  grown or cut as the bar in issue 201 is, only in brick proportions. They
  are true gem ruby, but every brick costs a whole crystal growth.
- **Sintered ruby-ceramic bricks.** Alumina powder with chromium oxide in
  it is pressed into brick moulds and fired below its melting point, so the
  grains fuse without the whole thing turning liquid. The result is red to
  pink, strong and heat-proof, but not transparent. Chrome-alumina
  refractory bricks of this kind already line glass furnaces. Translucent
  polycrystalline alumina also exists: it was used for the arc tubes of
  sodium street lamps.

These are the two ends of a scale. A castle's walls could be sintered
brick, with single-crystal "window rooms" set among them. The ruby-castle
vision mentions clear rooms.

## Suggested Implementation Steps

1. **Brick dimensions.** Choose a brick size and draw it.
2. **Sintered route.** Specify the powder recipe (alumina, chromium oxide
   content), the pressing, and the firing schedule, and say what colour and
   strength each produces.
3. **Crystal route.** Adapt the issue 201 furnace to brick proportions, and
   work out the cost per brick in energy and time.
4. **Phase 3 demo.** Show the two bricks side by side: dimensions, colour,
   clarity, and what each costs to make.
