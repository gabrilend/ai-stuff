# Setting: The Storm Castle

## The Premise

1944. The war in Europe takes a turn no one anticipated.

Intelligence reports filter through Allied command: Hitler has made contact with *something* in the Carpathian mountains. The castle of an ancient evil, long thought legend, has opened its gates to the Reich.

The US Army's 13th Airborne Division receives impossible orders: ascend the mountain, storm the castle, destroy whatever pact has been forged.

---

## The Marked List

Before deployment, command conducted a lottery. Half the soldiers' names were struck through in red marker - pre-designated casualties. Acceptable losses, determined before the mission began.

These marked soldiers were separated, given intensive training, and told exactly what they were: weapons to be driven into the enemy's throat. Suicide troops. They knew they weren't coming back.

The player controls the marked.

Every soldier who spawns was always meant to die here. The time loop doesn't change that expectation - it defies it. Each save, each faded ally pulled back from death, is a name being unmarked from the list.

The sergeant at boot camp wished them all luck: *"Because now the real work begins."*

A narrow stone bridge stretches from the mountain's military staging ground up to the castle's main gate. It rises at a steep angle, climbing through perpetual storm clouds. Lightning illuminates the path in strobing flashes.

### The Belltower

A lone skeletal structure on the right side of the causeway. Soldiers can climb it to gain a sniper vantage over the bridge, but the attached graveyard means constant harassment from below.

The tower provides:
- Clear sight lines to the bridge
- Elevated angle against flying enemies
- Exposure to graveyard threats

### The Graveyard

A small cemetery attached to the belltower's base. Skeletons rise endlessly from these graves, creating a persistent threat to any soldiers who take the sniper position.

### The Storm

Rain never stops. Thunder rolls continuously. Visibility is reduced to lightning flashes and muzzle fire. The darkness hides gargoyle approaches until the last moment.

## The Enemies

### Gargoyles
Stone sentries that detach from the castle parapets and dive-bomb soldiers. They move fast, hit hard, and shatter on impact - killing themselves and their target simultaneously.

**Behavior:** Spot a target, pause, dive in a straight line.
**Tell:** Brief pause and stone-grinding sound before dive.
**Counter:** Shoot during the pause, or dodge at the last moment.

### Skeletons
Endless infantry rising from the graveyard. Individually weak, dangerous in numbers. They shamble toward noise and movement.

**Behavior:** Rise, shamble toward nearest sound, swarm.
**Tell:** Grave soil disturbing, rattling bones.
**Counter:** Suppress with volume of fire, avoid clustering.

### Nether-bats
Small, fast flyers that impale soldiers from behind. They hunt the flanks and rear of advancing troops.

**Behavior:** Circle wide, strike from blind spots.
**Tell:** High-pitched screech seconds before attack.
**Counter:** Rear-guard positioning, listening for screech.

### Festus, the Aboleth

The main obstacle on the bridge. A massive floating monstrosity - a pockmarked mass of flesh buoyed by gas bladders, trailing dozens of eye-tipped tentacles.

**Behavior:** Hovers over the bridge, sweeps eye-beams across the causeway. Each beam turns a man to ash instantly.
**Tell:** Eyes glow brighter before firing.
**Counter:** This is the puzzle. Learning Festus's patterns is the key to reaching the gate.

## Tone and Atmosphere

### Color Palette
- Stone gray (castle, gargoyles, causeway)
- Sickly green (Festus's flesh, necrotic energy)
- Lightning white (strobing illumination)
- Deep black (shadows, the storm)
- Rust red (blood, old iron, the graveyard soil)

### Sound Design
- Constant rain and thunder
- Distant screams (from soldiers dying in other timelines?)
- Stone grinding (gargoyle movement)
- Bone rattle (skeleton emergence)
- Deep, wet breathing (Festus)

### Visual Style
the castle is cleaved stone blocks perfectly rectangularly sized. They are stacked so far that they touch the heavens, and the clouds gather around the apex, hiding what lies at it's center. a mountain-sized causeway rises up to meet it, and the base camp is less than a mile from it's summit. the stone blocks start about 3/4ths of the way up, and the rest is paved, like climbing up a shell. This is how far deep the castle goes, yet the bottom layers are empty aside from stone. the final strip splits in two, first the causeway that goes ~80% of the way up, and the final stretch of bridge guarded by Festus (by the way it can be different guardians at different times, it just depends on which we want to manually deplenish)

inside, the castle is a vast opening far beyond the confines of the castle outdoors. Inside of the very first room there is a series of balconies or awnings from which soldiers will move left/right at a run down the left/right pathway. These then split into three, upstairs downstairs and ahead of me. On the left path, there is emptiness to the right broken up by columns of solid gold with red velvet filligree - the floor is a plush red carpet bright as red can be with gold threaded embroidery along the edges. It is difficult to move quickly in here because the carpet is so thick, but the sides of the room are stone blocks so you can walk faster along those. However, up against the guardrail you are easy prey for your foes, and along the wall on the left there are hidden blade compartments that open and slaughter anyone they can. Their mortal remains soak the carpet.

The balconies mentioned look down upon a grand ballroom within which the demon rorcanoflex views them with his piercing stare. Should he move his bright violet eyeball near one of the balconies, all within are turned to stone. He does this at their endpoint, where he can gaze directly down the hallway and statue-ize them all. Eventually, it becomes so thick that the soldiers must bring pickaxes. But, should they take enough potshots at him while he crosses left to right, they can bleed him until the stone is slick with his blood and he falls and breaks every single one of his bones at once. There he rests as a quivering hunger of forced rotten humility as penance for his wicked deeds uncountably untold.

The next room has monsters made out of wax. The bullets pierce through and eventually the wax loses it's forms and coalesces into a gooey lead snake. This snake will vanish all of your senses, and leave you blind, mumbling, and in disarray as your comrades struggle to step over you in the slick yet confining wax and bullet malaise. This snake grows bigger the more bullets it can pull into it's form, but the wax hardens with cold so when the torches in the room are snuffed out, by explosive fire or manual covering, the wax becomes like caked snow and no more momentum is possible. At this time any lead snakes created are no longer able to grow, and can be pinned down with forked stakes and obliterated. Oh, and there are mind-enslaved cultists that need to get shot before they plunge their rippling daggers (as if something partway out of spacetime) into the hearts of those brave soldier warriors.

evil knows no dimension. it must be contested with wits and heart and forces.

"where is this evil?" in our hearts, of course.

the next room has not yet been discovered.

## The Question

Every soldier who marches up that causeway asks the same thing:

*"Why am I here again?"*

They don't remember dying. They don't know they're echoes. But something in them knows this isn't their first time up that bridge.

Deja vu. A flash of a gargoyle that hasn't arrived yet. The certainty that the next lightning strike will reveal something terrible.

The soldiers don't understand the time loop.
But they feel it.
