# Narrative Introduction: The Marked List

## The Boot Camp Scene

*[INTRO CINEMATIC]*

A drill sergeant stands before rows of fresh recruits. He holds a clipboard. Half the names are struck through in red marker.

---

**SERGEANT:**
"Alright. I got a list here of all of your names, and I picked half of them randomly to be stricken through with a marker in red.

These are the people who are dead.

You will have died in action, and you will never have known which of you would live or die.

Now, let me read off the names:

A. Aaronson: dead.
B. Briansson: alive.
C. Cameron: alive..."

*[TIME PASSES - the sergeant works through the alphabet]*

**SERGEANT:**
"...okay. Now, if I read your name off as dead, follow me. We're going to assign you a suicide mission.

You'll enter intensive training - the best we got - and we'll make you into a weapon to drive into the throats of our enemies.

Good luck, everyone."

**RECRUIT:**
"Wait... you said 'good luck' at the end?"

**SERGEANT:**
"Yep. Because now the real work begins."

*[CUT TO BLACK]*

---

## Design Implications

### The Marked Soldiers

Every soldier the player controls was pre-selected for death. They knew it before they shipped out. They trained knowing they wouldn't return.

This reframes the time loop:
- **Without the loop:** They die as intended. Mission complete. Acceptable losses.
- **With the loop:** The player defies the list. Unmarking the dead.

### The "Weapon" Training

The marked soldiers received "the best training we got." This explains:
- Why the player has combat competence from run 1
- Why the soldiers can perform precise interventions
- Why they don't panic when they see impossible things (faded allies, timeline echoes)

They were made into weapons. Weapons don't question.

### "Good Luck, Everyone"

The sergeant wishes luck to *everyone* - dead and alive alike. Because:
- The alive must live with surviving the selection
- The dead must accomplish something worth dying for
- Both face "the real work"

This is not cruelty. This is honesty about what war demands.

---

## Cinematic Integration Points

### Opening
The boot camp scene plays before the title card. The player doesn't know yet that they are one of the marked.

### First Death
When the player dies for the first time and sees the recording, a flash: their name on the clipboard. Red marker through it. They were always going to die here.

### First Save
When the player saves a past self from death, another flash: the red marker line fading from a name. The list can be rewritten.

### True Victory
Upon achieving zero casualties, the final shot: the clipboard. Every name unmarked. The sergeant's voice:

*"Well. Looks like I was wrong about all of you."*

---

## Thematic Weight

The list introduces predestination. The loop introduces free will.

The tension between these creates the game's moral core:
- Are you defying fate, or fulfilling a different one?
- If you *can* save everyone, are you obligated to?
- What does it mean that someone decided these deaths were acceptable before they happened?

The pyrrhic victory isn't just about casualties. It's about accepting a system that pre-marks the dead. True victory rejects that system entirely.

---

## Notes for Implementation

The boot camp scene should be:
- Black and white or desaturated (memory/flashback aesthetic)
- Static shots, minimal movement
- Sergeant's voice flat, matter-of-fact (this is routine to him)
- Sound design: boots shuffling, paper rustling, distant artillery

The clipboard should be a recurring visual motif throughout the game.
